Generated by `Scripts/profiling/PartA_EDA.ipynb` from `gs://cnde-ev-raw-group3/clean/latest`.
Supply = open, public Level 2 + DC fast ports (findings 1-3: today; findings 4-7: open by the end of 2025). Demand = registered EVs at the end of 2025.

| # | Finding | Evidence | What it means for the project |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chargers are concentrated in a few states | CA has 26% of public ports; the top 5 states have 50% | Compare states with a ratio (EVs per port), not raw counts |
| 2 | Most stations are small, and fast charging is a minority | Median 2 ports per station; 76% have 1-2 ports; DC fast = 30% of public ports | The gap score should weigh DC fast ports separately |
| 3 | A few networks run most ports | ChargePoint Network has 33%; the top 3 have 56% | Network is a useful dashboard filter |
| 4 | EVs are growing faster than public chargers | From 2016 to 2025, EVs grew 20.3x and public ports 17.7x; EVs per port went from 21.6 to 24.8 | The gap is widening: this supports the business problem |
| 5 | The gap differs a lot by state | US average 24.8 EVs per port. Largest: NJ (37.0), HI (36.3), FL (33.9), TX (33.9), NV (33.3). Smallest: WY (6.0), ND (6.7), ME (8.4) | These are the first candidates for the top-5 recommendation |
| 6 | Moderate positive relationship between electricity price and EV adoption | Pearson r = 0.44, Spearman r = 0.50 (without Hawaii: 0.48) | States with higher prices tend to have MORE EVs, likely because coastal, higher-income states have both. Price does not hold back adoption, so use it only as a cost factor in the gap score, not as a demand signal |
| 7 | California is an outlier | 32% of US EVs and 27% of public ports | Show it, but use ratios so it does not dominate; consider a view without CA |
| 8 | Registration method changed in 2021 | Biodiesel 0 to 2,194,100; CNG 521,600 to 40,800 (2020 to 2021) | Use only EV and PHEV counts across years |

Limitations: closed stations are not in the API, so ports by opening year undercount early years; registration counts are rounded to the nearest 100.
